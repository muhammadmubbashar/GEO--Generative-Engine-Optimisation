1. Improving Flight Centre’s use as an AI source

65 seconds · Evidence C01, C19, C22

Our project asks how Flight Centre can improve its visibility in AI-assisted travel shopping. Semester One identified a broad set of opportunities. This semester we implemented and tested one specific part: how the page content supplied to an assistant affects whether that page is cited. Our research question is: when relevant Flight Centre information is already available alongside competitors, can a clearer representation increase its use as a source? We built a laboratory, tested individual methods, assembled a combined page package and preserved the evidence. Today we will explain the design, show actual changes and results, and distinguish what we demonstrated from what remains unproven. The presentation is twenty minutes, with questions afterwards.

Transition: Next: Source citation, recommendation and customer action.

2. Source citation, recommendation and customer action

60 seconds · Evidence C01, C20

Visibility can mean several things. A page can be available to the assistant; the answer can cite it; the answer can recommend the business; and a customer can act. These are different outcomes. Our main measurement is the second one. We count an answer once if it contains a valid reference to a Flight Centre source, whether it cites that source once or several times. A citation gives the business an opportunity to be seen as a source of useful information. It does not, by itself, prove that the assistant recommended Flight Centre for booking, or that a traveller clicked or purchased.

Transition: Next: Our retrieval-augmented generation workflow.

3. Our retrieval-augmented generation workflow

75 seconds · Evidence C03, C09

Our experiment uses a retrieval-augmented generation workflow, often called RAG. First, our pipeline selects saved source pages. Next, it puts numbered source text and the traveller’s question into a prompt. The language model then writes an answer and chooses references from that supplied evidence. Finally, the evaluator maps those reference numbers back to the saved sources. The important distinction is who controls what. Our code controls the available evidence, its order and the common instruction. The model chooses the wording and which available sources to cite. In this fixed branch there is no live browsing during generation. The prompt asks for supporting citations after each sentence, but never requires Flight Centre to be cited. This is a controlled source-use test, not a measurement of an unconstrained consumer assistant.

Transition: Next: A matched comparison of two reading packs.

4. A matched comparison of two reading packs

70 seconds · Evidence C02, C03, C09, C10

Three search variants were merged into saved candidate pools. Of forty primary questions, twenty-nine had a Flight Centre page in the first-ten analysis pool and were eligible for this comparison. We then prepared two reading packs for each question: the baseline and the treatment. The source identities, source order, competitor texts and instruction stayed matched. Only the Flight Centre representation changed. Two source arrangements and five repeats produced ten observations per question, or two hundred and ninety per condition. We verified those matches directly in the saved records. The baseline answers are historical, rather than freshly generated alongside the treatment. This limits causal certainty. The result also says nothing directly about the eleven questions where FC was outside the fixed analysis pool.

5. Individual methods: results and interpretation

85 seconds · Evidence C13, C14, C15, C16

These six method families were explored separately. Rendered content exposes captured offers and semantic cleanup keeps labels and digits intelligible. Grounded FAQ pairs supported questions with answers. Structured facts organise existing information into fields. These three are intended to improve how the model reads information. External enrichment adds attributed outside material; schema adds structure; rewriting changes the wording more broadly. All six rows here use the same thirty-nine-point-three-percent historical control. FAQ and typed facts have positive exploratory intervals; rendering’s interval crosses zero. External enrichment does not show a clear isolated gain. The schema experiment also changed visible text, so its improvement cannot be assigned to JSON-LD. Automatic rewriting declined. These are unadjusted exploratory comparisons across multiple versions, not a definitive ranking or six proven ingredients.

6. Selection of the combined treatment

45 seconds · Evidence C13, C14, C15, C16

We did not combine every method or add their percentage gains together. The selected package uses captured rendered content, semantic cleanup, joint grounded facts and questions, and a final FAQ extraction refinement. Added external enrichment, added schema, broad rewriting and source positioning remain separate evidence. The choice considers implementation and compatibility as well as scores. The typed-facts variant in the preceding table is not identical to every fact component later used in the package. The combined version must therefore be tested as its own treatment. We cannot assign a particular share of its improvement to each component.

9. Restoring a captured FAQ question

70 seconds · Evidence C11, C12

This example shows a more tightly controlled change. The page contained a question inside an accordion button. Our common cleaner removed interactive buttons, so the question label could disappear even when its expanded answer remained. The refinement converts an eligible captured question label into readable text and keeps it next to the same answer. It does not invent an answer. It only handles clearly paired, already-expanded sections. The before-and-after view shows why an answer without its question can be less useful, and how restoring that relationship improves the representation sent to the model.

10. Implementation checks and their limits

65 seconds · Evidence C09, C10, C11, C12

We checked input identity, transformation invariants and delivery to the model. The final FAQ code only converts supported, already-expanded question controls, and checks that the existing answers, offer cards, metadata and links remain unchanged. We rebuilt that stage from the supplied prior-core input and matched all fifty-two saved page outputs. We also compared all three hundred and thirty FC source occurrences in the selected generated records against those outputs under the character cap: every occurrence matches. The submission includes the code and offline checker. These are strong implementation checks, but they have a precise scope. They do not certify every inherited fact, remove capture-date differences, or establish that the same pages would be selected by a public AI engine.

12. The same pages improved citation in two runs

70 seconds · Evidence C04, C05, C06, C07

Here are both generations of the unchanged combined pages. The saved baseline contains one hundred and sixteen citing answers out of two hundred and ninety: forty percent. The initial treatment run contains one hundred and forty-three: forty-nine-point-three percent. The repeat contains one hundred and fifty-two: fifty-two-point-four percent. Relative to the same baseline, those are increases of nine-point-three and twelve-point-four percentage points. We verified that the initial and repeat treatment prompts and sources were identical. The answers still differ because generation varies. Showing both runs is more informative than presenting the higher run as a stable expected rate. Both are development evidence; neither is an independent, untouched confirmation on new customer needs.

Transition: Next: Question-level uncertainty and variation.

13. Question-level uncertainty and variation

70 seconds · Evidence C06, C08

Repeated answers to one question are related, so we analyse uncertainty by resampling whole questions while keeping their ten observations together. In the independently recalculated analysis, the initial gain has a ninety-five-percent interval from about three-point-one to fifteen-point-nine percentage points. The repeat gain has an interval from about five-point-two to twenty. Both are positive in these saved comparisons. However, the difference between the two treatment runs has an interval crossing zero. We should not claim the repeat is better. The repeat also improves twenty-one question averages, declines on six and ties on two. These intervals do not correct for trying many versions or using a historical control. Our conclusion remains exploratory.

Transition: Next: Source-order sensitivity.

14. Source-order sensitivity

65 seconds · Evidence C17

We also checked source ordering. In the corrected position experiment, source text stayed the same, the first Flight Centre source moved to the beginning, and competitor relative order was preserved. The rate moved from thirty-nine-point-three to fifty-seven-point-six percent. This suggests our measured visibility is sensitive to where evidence appears inside the model’s reading pack. It is consistent with research showing that models can use context differently depending on position. It does not show that Flight Centre can control a public engine’s internal source order, and position manipulation was not included in the final combined page package.

Transition: Next: Practical value for Flight Centre.

15. Practical value for Flight Centre

65 seconds · Evidence C19, C20, C22

The practical output is a working content-transformation prototype and a way to test its effect. Flight Centre could use the approach to make existing route information, conditions and useful answers easier to extract, while maintaining a governed product source. The business value demonstrated here is a better opportunity for source credit when that information is already in the assistant’s reading pack. We have not demonstrated more booking recommendations or sales. A live application would need current prices, content ownership and separate measurement of discovery, supported recommendations and customer actions. Those are proposed applications and evaluation steps, not additional achievements we are claiming for this semester.

16. Scope of the conclusion

70 seconds · Evidence C06, C18, C20, C22

The selected fixed comparisons are positive, but broader evidence limits the conclusion. The separate reindexed run was essentially unchanged, and fresh rewordings showed a positive estimate whose interval crossed zero. The corpus-search exercise did not establish increased booking recommendations. These tasks have different source-exposure conditions, so their percentages cannot be pooled. Our main design also uses historical controls, captures from different contexts, related tasks and a single saved model configuration. Earlier results informed later development. Semester One proposed a wider evaluation programme, including relevance and calibrated judging, which this headline does not establish. The strongest conclusion is higher observed source-citation inclusion for the selected representation under the tested fixed conditions.

18. Contribution and defensible conclusion

60 seconds · Evidence C04, C05, C06, C19, C20, C21

We finish with three contributions. First, we built an operational experiment with preserved sources and matched comparisons. Second, we implemented and checked a coherent page package, learning from both successful and unsuccessful approaches. Third, we supplied an evidence trail that allows the reported citation counts and the final refinement to be independently checked. The unchanged combined pages produced forty-nine-point-three and fifty-two-point-four-percent citation rates against a forty-percent historical baseline. This is positive exploratory evidence of conditional source visibility. It is not proof of increased recommendations or revenue. 22. Published research: what it supports

0 seconds · Evidence C01–C23

RAG provides the idea of supplying retrieved evidence during generation. GEO studies source modifications and visibility. ALCE separates the presence of references from whether they correctly and fully support an answer. Lost in the Middle motivates checking how context order affects performance. These are relevant foundations with different tasks and metrics. Our binary answer-level inclusion rate is not identical to GEO’s visibility measures, and we did not reproduce a learned AutoGEO system. Public agency offerings add an operational checklist, not audited evidence that their advertised methods work for FC.
